### Lessons

- **The worktrees started from `origin/main` (c8c49f6, 2026-10-22), not local `main`**, 25 commits behind. Every fix was made and verified on day-old code. The two good fixes cherry-picked cleanly and the full suite on main matched the baseline. From round 2 on, each agent first resets its worktree to local main.
- **The three unmerged fixes rested on hand-written phrase lists** (death words; ~45 harm verbs, body parts and person words; a parse of the verifier's wording). The skeptics showed what CLAUDE.md predicts: ordinary phrasings missed (`slit`, `put a bullet in`, `kill` vs `killed`), false hits (`i cut the guard off mid-sentence` rolled a melee attack; `i hit the guard with a question`), and prose cut instead of state reconciled. Fix by class: candidates come from the engine's own records (every person and place code the turn names), and one small plain model ask decides.
- **One interpreter, not three detectors.** #154 (deaths), #129/#85 (outcome vs dice), #157 (memory) and #41 (gold taken with no payment) are one class: the turn's outcome is never stated as facts. CLAUDE.md already plans it (narrator, interpreter, engine). Round 2 builds its core; dice and gold build on its events in round 3.
- **New asks must log their prompt, reply and timing** in the turn trace; round 1's asks passed `trace=[]` / `usage=[]` like ask_weight and sketches, so a live failure could not be debugged.
- **A recorded death must be undoable** (rewind, and a later turn that shows a revival the world allows), or one false yes from the 8B kills an NPC for good.

It is short and plain, which is allowed under 2026-10-21/22, but it has not been checked live on the 8B, and the brief admits its yes-bias risk. **facts**

- The hold asks one question per dead person and, on one yes, cuts every grammar-flagged sentence together. That includes corpse descriptions and the player's own act sentences ('You kneel beside Beth [[B]], her eyes still open.' and 'Beth [[B]] lies where she fell, motionless.' are flagged as acts), so a single yes about one living line can take out the player's own act.
- A recorded death cannot be undone except by rewind. A false yes from the 8B on a candidate such as 'Beth [[B]] kneels beside the corpse of a raider' or 'Beth [[B]] killed the raider' (both are candidates) leaves an NPC dead for good, and every later line where she acts is cut.
- A death written by someone else in a two-person sentence is pinned on the wrong person ('Jonah grabs Beth. She dies in his arms.' makes Jonah the candidate), so the ask can be asked about the wrong NPC.
- Matches one phrasing: DEATH_RE/RUIN_RE carry run-specific phrases ('(ripped|torn|tears|rips) ... (apart|in half|in two)', 'life has been ripped', 'neck twisted', 'melting ... to nothing', 'reduced to'). The basic class words are missing ('kill', 'kills', 'execute', 'collapses', 'explodes', 'burn X to the ground' with the object in between). The candidate step is phrase-fitted to cc87dd29 rather than built for the class.
- The grammar-based act finder in act_sentences is a large hand-built regex set (title words, clause openers, quote tracking). It already misses inverted speech tags and unnamed speakers, so more phrase rules are likely to pile up there, which is the outcome the 2026-10-14 'fix by class, not by phrasing' decision was meant to avoid.
- Gate rather than reconcile: on yes, the hold cuts sentences with no retry and no reconcile. In T13 the turn shrinks to one 84-char sentence. The rule allows a gate for facts that contradict the state, but cutting several sentences on one yes, including corpse descriptions and the player's own acts, comes close to limiting the prose.
- No way back: a recorded death is permanent outside rewind. Raising the dead, ghouls, or simply a mistaken record is then gated forever, which limits the model and the player.
- No narrator prompt instruction was added, which complies. The facts go through the existing facts_to_keep data line.

The attempts are kept on branches `worktree-wf_c97ad18c-f79-3` (facts), `-4` (dice), `-5` (memory-checker) for reference.

## Live measurement of the asks (2026-10-24)

One agent measured all 17 new asks on Hermes 3 8B: docs/showcase/ask-live-eval-2026-10-24.md. KEEP: died, destroyed, returned, entity_kind, speaks, choice, where_ended (all 100%), retell (73%, low risk). SIMPLIFY: hold (64% -> 93% as "Does <Name> say or do anything in it?"), hold_which (58% -> 88% one sentence at a time), reply (55% -> 100% without the negation "did not do this"), voices (77% -> 85%). DROP: every "rewrite this sentence" ask (usable 36-39%) and the checker's act check (75% false yes).

Lessons:
- **Every ask costs 0.05-0.56 s on the GPU.** Asks can run on every turn; keyword pre-filters to decide when to ask are not needed, and the hand-written trigger lists the skeptics objected to can go.
- **The 8B is good at yes/no about one named thing in a short passage; bad at picking from lists, at negated questions, at "does the scene show what the player wanted" (it says yes), and at rewriting.** Shape every ask as a positive yes/no about one name or one sentence.
- **Temperature 0 is not fully repeatable** here: one identical prompt gave two different number lists depending on what ran before it. Never let one answer change state alone.
- **An opening fact can bias the answer:** "Beth died in turn N." before the question pulled the hold ask to "no" on living cases.
